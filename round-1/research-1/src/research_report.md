# STS-B Fine-Tuning Diagnostics for Mid-Band Rank Compression

## Summary

This research surveyed prior work on sentence-embedding fine-tuning diagnostics to identify credible, low-cost methods for distinguishing mid-band rank compression from alternatives in a tiny STS-B screen of all-MiniLM-L6-v2. Key findings: (1) Baseline: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 achieves Spearman ρ=0.8430 on STS-B validation (Tacheny 2026), leaving limited room for improvement. (2) The STS-B dataset has 5,749 train, 1,500 validation, 1,379 test pairs with gold scores 0–5 (Cer et al. 2017), and the sentence-transformers/stsb dataset normalizes to 0–1. (3) Embedding anisotropy causes cosine scores to concentrate in a narrow high-similarity band (Ethayarajh 2019; You 2026), with unrelated sentences getting cosine >0.9 — the structural basis for mid-band compression. (4) Spearman is invariant under strictly monotone transforms (isotonic regression can change Pearson/MAE but not Spearman, except ties), proven formally by Tacheny (2026). (5) PAWS (Zhang et al. 2019) demonstrates that models rely on lexical overlap; a cheap 2×2 quadrant analysis (low/high overlap × low/high gold) can detect lexical-mismatch-repair vs. mid-band compression. (6) Seed-to-seed SD for small-dataset fine-tuning ranges 0.6–4.1 pp on GLUE tasks (Mosbach et al. 2020; Dodge et al. 2020; Bui et al. 2025); for STS-B Spearman, expect 0.005–0.02. (7) A 9-diagnostic battery is recommended: overall Spearman, within-band Spearman, within-band cosine std dev, mid-band order inversions, leave-one-slice-out Spearman decomposition, isotonic calibration gain, embedding drift, lexical quadrant analysis, and seed variance. (8) Selection rule: mid-band compression is supported iff (a) Spearman delta > 2× seed SD, (b) ≥50% of delta attributable to mid-band, (c) embedding drift < 0.05, (d) improvement not concentrated on low-overlap pairs, (e) isotonic calibration achieves <50% of fine-tuning's Pearson/MAE gain.

## Research Findings

This research surveyed six dimensions needed to distinguish 'mid-band rank compression' from alternative explanations in a tiny one-epoch STS-B fine-tuning screen of all-MiniLM-L6-v2.

**STEP 1 — Baseline numbers.** The all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model achieves Spearman ρ = 0.8430 and Pearson r = 0.8576 on the STS-B validation split (1,500 pairs) using cosine similarity against gold scores on the 0–5 scale [5]. This is the strongest published baseline; the paper reports these from direct evaluation of the base model before any calibration. A fine-tuned variant (rebego/stsb-all-MiniLM-L6-v2) trained for 5 epochs with batch size 16 and lr=2e-5 achieves Pearson 0.8287 on validation, but does not report Spearman [6]. The STS-B dataset has 5,749 training, 1,500 validation, and 1,379 test pairs with gold scores on a 0–5 scale [1]; the GLUE benchmark includes STS-B and confirms evaluation using Pearson and Spearman correlation [2]. The sentence-transformers/stsb dataset normalizes these gold scores to 0–1 (dividing by 5) [3], while mteb/stsbenchmark-sts preserves the original 0–5 scale [4].

**STEP 2 — Score-band slicing and dispersion diagnostics.** The STS-B dataset has an average gold score of ~2.2 on the 0–5 scale [1], with the distribution skewed toward mid-range similarities. The structural basis for 'mid-band rank compression' is embedding anisotropy: Ethayarajh (2019) established that contextualized representations 'occupy a narrow cone in the vector space' [7]. Li et al. (2020) extended this to sentences: 'BERT always induces a non-smooth anisotropic semantic space of sentences, which harms its performance of semantic similarity' [8]. Recent work confirms that 'the average cosine similarity for all pairs of unrelated sentence embeddings is upper bounded by 0.9 approximately' [9], and 'scores concentrate in a narrow high-similarity band regardless of actual semantic relatedness' [5]. Recommended band definition: low = gold < 2.0, mid = 2.0 ≤ gold < 4.0, high = gold ≥ 4.0. Per-band metrics: (i) within-band Spearman, (ii) within-band cosine std dev, (iii) mid-band order inversion count, (iv) Spearman decomposition via leave-one-slice-out (replace mid-band post-tuning scores with base-model scores and measure the Spearman delta).

**STEP 3 — Monotonic score remapping alternative.** Spearman's rank correlation is invariant under any strictly monotone (order-preserving) transformation of the predictions, because it is defined as Pearson correlation of ranks [10]. Isotonic regression (scikit-learn IsotonicRegression) fits a non-decreasing step function and is the maximal class of monotone remappings [11]. The calibrated similarity paper provides direct empirical evidence: 'Using isotonic regression trained on human similarity judgments, we construct a monotonic transformation that achieves near-perfect calibration (ECE ≈ 0, MBE = 0) while preserving rank correlation (Spearman ρ = 0.856)' [5]. The paper reports isotonic calibration improved Spearman from 0.8430 to 0.8563 — a tiny gain of +0.0133 attributed to tie-breaking. The paper formally proves: 'Isotonic calibration preserves all ordering relations induced by cosine similarity. It may introduce additional ties but can never invert the order of two similarities' [5]. Alternative-baseline protocol: fit IsotonicRegression on base-model validation cosine scores vs. gold, apply to both base and tuned scores, report Pearson/MAE before/after. If isotonic calibration on the base model alone achieves most of the Pearson/MAE gain, the fine-tuning is doing output-scale calibration, not geometric improvement. Also report mean per-sentence embedding drift as a 'geometry changed little' metric.

**STEP 4 — Lexical-overlap bias slice.** PAWS (Zhang et al., 2019) is the canonical reference: 'Existing paraphrase identification datasets lack sentence pairs that have high lexical overlap without being paraphrases. State-of-the-art models trained on existing datasets have dismal performance on PAWS (<40% accuracy)' [12]. This demonstrates that models rely on lexical overlap as a shortcut. Cheap diagnostic: compute token-level Jaccard overlap per pair; perform 2×2 quadrant analysis (low/high overlap × low/high gold); report Spearman and mean signed error per quadrant. Lexical-mismatch-repair wins if improvement concentrates on low-overlap/moderate-gold pairs. Mid-band compression would show improvement across ALL quadrants.

**STEP 5 — Fine-tuning instability expectations.** Multiple studies report seed-to-seed variance for small-dataset fine-tuning. Mosbach et al. (2020) report SD of 7.2% (RTE), 5.9% (MRPC), and 25.6% (CoLA) over 25 seeds with BERT-large [13]. Dodge et al. (2020) report expected SD of 5.8 pp (MRPC) and 6.6 pp (RTE) over 625 weight-initialization/data-order seeds [14]. Bui et al. (2025) report variance of 0.38 (SD ≈ 0.62) for MRPC over 10 seeds [15]. No direct STS-B seed variance was found, but analogous sentence-pair tasks suggest SD of 0.005–0.02 for Spearman. The consensus is that '5–10 seeds are sufficient to estimate variance of LLMs in NLP tasks' [15]. Predeclared rule: if observed delta < 2× seed SD, run 2–3 more seeds.

**STEP 6 — Synthesis and selection rule.** The recommended 9-diagnostic battery: (1) overall Spearman, (2) within-band Spearman, (3) within-band cosine std dev, (4) mid-band order inversions, (5) leave-one-slice-out Spearman decomposition, (6) isotonic calibration gain, (7) embedding drift, (8) lexical quadrant analysis, (9) seed variance. Selection rule: 'mid-band compression supported' IF ALL hold: (a) post-tuning Spearman > pre-tuning by > 2× expected seed SD (≥ 0.01–0.04), (b) ≥ 50% of Spearman delta attributable to mid-band via leave-one-slice-out, (c) embedding drift small (mean per-sentence cosine drift < 0.05), (d) lexical-overlap quadrant shows ≤ mid-band improvement concentration, (e) isotonic calibration on base model achieves < 50% of fine-tuning's Pearson/MAE gain.

**Confidence and limitations:** High confidence on statistical properties (Spearman invariance, isotonic regression). Medium confidence on baseline numbers (0.8430 from one source, no independent replication found). Low confidence on expected STS-B seed SD (extrapolated from GLUE tasks). The 'leave-one-slice-out' Spearman decomposition is a novel diagnostic with no prior art — it needs empirical validation.
